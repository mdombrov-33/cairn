How much do you want to drive?

This sets who handles the busywork — you can loosen or tighten any single control later, in settings, mid-campaign.

Full controlEvery roll, every reaction, every companion decision passes through you. Slowest, most tabletop.
Tactical ✓You fight your own fights and make your own rolls; companions run themselves and routine checks resolve quietly. Where most tables land.
Narrator's tableYou speak, the DM handles the mechanics off-screen. Closest to reading a story you're inside of.
What does dying mean here?

Locked once the campaign starts — the one setting you don't get to renegotiate at 0 HP.

Story sparesGoing down always costs something — captured, scarred, robbed — but never the character. Death only happens if you write it.
By the book ✓Death saves, as written. Three fails and the river keeps you — but the dice have to say so.
HardcoreOne grave. No death saves, no return — the campaign ends where the character does, and the record stays.
How dark should the telling get?

Same story either way — this shapes how the narrator describes what happens, not what can happen.

Lantern-litDanger is real but the camera cuts away. Violence lands off-screen; dread over gore.
Grim ✓Wounds bleed and losses are described honestly, without lingering. The Riverlands as written.
GraveyardNothing softened. Folk horror at full strength — for tables that want to be unsettled.
Anything the story should never touch?

Written in your own words. These are hard lines — the narrator won't put them on screen at any tone setting, ever.

harm to children; anything with drowning described slowly
